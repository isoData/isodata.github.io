The Sinclair Job

The rain is a steady, unforgiving drumbeat on the window of your office. A small, crumpled note on your desk details the case: **Evelyn Sinclair**, missing for 48 hours. No witnesses, no ransom, just a locked apartment and a chill that doesn't come from the December air.

"She was a dame with expensive taste and a reputation to match," the voice on the wire had said, rasping like cheap whiskey. "Check the usual haunts first, Detective. She liked the bright lights, even if they were just reflecting off the puddles."

You stub out your cigarette. The city is waiting for its answer, and the night is young and dangerous. Two starting points stand out from her phone records—a high-end jazz club and a secluded, downtown art gallery.
